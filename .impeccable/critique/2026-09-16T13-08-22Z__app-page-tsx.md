## Overall Impression

This is a strong, restrained foundation. Its biggest opportunity is to make proof—not biography—the visual peak. A visitor should see one distinctive system, result, or architectural artifact within the first viewport and understand why Otis is different before reading the full introduction.

## What's Working

- The editorial palette, typography, and divider system feel deliberate in both themes.
- The simplified project rows now scan logically: company, role, context, evidence, and action.
- Responsive structure and semantics are strong: one H1, one H2, three H3s, labeled regions, skip link, visible focus, and no overflow.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Concrete proof arrives too late

**Why it matters:** At a representative desktop viewport, selected work sits below the fold while the headline and biography dominate the entire first screen. The most persuasive result, such as the 80% test-speed improvement, is several levels down in supporting copy.

**Fix:** Reduce the vertical footprint of the intro or bring a featured project proof point into the masthead. Let the first selected-work row peek into the initial viewport, or place one compact outcome/architecture preview beside the biography.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### [P1] The homepage lacks its promised signature artifact

**Why it matters:** The design contract positions inspectable architecture as the memorable moment, but the homepage is entirely textual. This makes an otherwise refined page feel like an elegant resume template rather than a portfolio for a systems engineer.

**Fix:** Feature one compact architecture flow, project capture, or inspectable system strip for the lead case study. Keep the remaining projects as concise editorial rows so the visual hierarchy reflects project importance.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable bolder`

### [P2] Project evidence is too uniformly weighted

**Why it matters:** Three highlights, six technology tags, description, metadata, and CTA appear for every project with similar visual emphasis. The project list is compact, but key outcomes still compete with implementation details.

**Fix:** Promote one dominant proof point per project, demote or defer the technology list, and let secondary evidence move into the case study. Consider showing three core technologies plus a count rather than six small tags.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P2] Mobile navigation and the ending weaken conversion

**Why it matters:** On mobile the Work label disappears, GitHub becomes icon-only, and several header targets measure below 44×44px. After the final project, the page ends immediately in copyright, leaving a convinced visitor without a strong next step.

**Fix:** Preserve a compact labeled Work path, enlarge header touch areas, and add a short closing contact block after the project list.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`
